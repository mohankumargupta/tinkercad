### Step 3: select both shapes

<Image src={platewithhole3} alt="Select shapes" />

Two ways to select shape:

1. Start outside both shapes, then left click and drag, you will notice a dashed box appear. Make 
   sure the dashed box at least partly cover both shapes.
2. Using keyboard shortcuts, select one shape, then press **Shift**, then press the other shape.

You will know it worked when you see **Shapes(2)** in the Shape Options/Properties panel/window.

### Step 4: use the align tool to center the cylinder in the rectangle

<Image src={platewithhole4} alt="Plate with hole" />

Now that both shapes are selected, click the align tool. <Image src={align} alt="align" /> The greyed out ones in the image are the
ones you need to click. The fact that they are greyed out is a visual indication that the shape is
centered in that axis.

:::tip[Pro Tip]
Normally when you align two shapes after clicking align tool, both shapes physically move.

Sometimes, you want one object(eg. the box) to be stationary while the other object(eg. the cylinder)
moves to the correct alignment.

To achieve this, immediately click on the stationary object after clicking the align tool.
You will notice the dots surround the stationary object. Then make your alignments. The result
will be that the cylinder moves to the stationary box.
:::
